3. Pass entry expanded — factual, no verdict
States what changed and offers a way back in, but never tells the user what to conclude — no "you were wrong to pass" framing. A buy is finished, so its entry just links to current status; a pass is unfinished, so "is it still worth passing" stays a live, current question with its own comparison panel.

8. Backdated purchase — no grace window, dual dates shown
No arbitrary "too many days" cutoff. The snapshot is always real data captured at the moment of logging — never claimed to be from the purchase date. When the two dates differ, both are shown as structural facts side by side, not a skippable footnote — the mismatch is self-evident from the layout itself.

10. Adding to an existing position — its own entry, own evidence
Deliberately not merged into the original buy entry. "Added to" language (not "Bought") signals a follow-on decision, not a first purchase. Shows the running share count and the new average cost, reusing the "averaging up/down" language from the buy planner. Its own fresh evidence snapshot, since months passed and the picture isn't the same as the original buy.
